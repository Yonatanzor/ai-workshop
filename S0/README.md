# Session 0 — Introduction & Simulation Day

**Type:** Half-day workshop (afternoon)  
**Audience:** First-semester MD-PhD students  
**Data tier:** Tier 1 (public tools, public sources only)

---

## What students leave with

A working understanding of when AI is and isn't the right tool, hands-on experience with two evidence-retrieval tools (Perplexity and QED Science), and the first entry in their failure log.

## Learning outcomes

- Distinguish evidence-*retrieval* AI tools (Perplexity, OpenEvidence) from reasoning-*critique* tools (QED Science)
- Identify at least two ways an AI tool can mislead a careful reader
- Decide, for a given research or clinical task, whether AI or a traditional tool is the better choice

## Session structure

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Group formation (mix of biology and CS backgrounds — intentional) |
| 0–5 min | Register for Perplexity and QED Science with institutional email |
| Part 1 (75 min) | Applied prompting and evidence-grounded AI tools |
| Part 2 | Build a small AI tool — first exposure to APIs |

## Before the session

Students should have read the assigned background material on how language models produce text (prediction, not lookup) and the difference between machine learning and deep learning.

## Critical-thinking challenge

Students receive four AI-generated claims from medical news stories or abstracts. For each, they write the evidence they would need before believing it, guided by the red-flag checklist: accuracy above 99%, no external test set, no comparison with a simple baseline.
