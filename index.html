The Five Questions

Students ask these of every tool, in every session, until they are a habit. Critical thinking is practised, not lectured: each session's challenge is built around how that day's tool fails.

01
What was it trained on, or given to read? Identify the data the model has access to — and what it doesn't.
02
How would I know if this is wrong? Every output needs a verification strategy before it is used.
03
Who or what is missing from its data? Gaps in training data become gaps in outputs — sometimes invisible ones.
04
What did using it cost — privacy, time, or a skill I did not practise? Every tool use has a cost beyond the subscription fee.
05
Would a non-AI method do this better? AI is not always the right tool. The four-test card sort applies here too.
Data Confidentiality
Course rule: no identifiable patient data and no unpublished lab data in any public AI tool. Students check, for each tool, whether they are signed in with a personal or an institutional account, and whether inputs may be used for training.
Tier 1
Published papers & public datasets
Any approved tool. Spot-check the output and its sources.
Tier 2
De-identified research data
Structure only; code runs where the data lives. Verify results a second way.
Tier 3
Identifiable patient or unpublished lab data
Nothing in public tools. Approve every input and output; access is logged.
Tier 4
Anything affecting a patient decision
Drafts only, never actions. A qualified clinician decides and stays accountable.
