Learning Objectives

By the end of this session you will be able to:

  1. Distinguish evidence-retrieval AI tools from reasoning-critique tools
  2. Identify at least two ways an AI tool can mislead a careful reader
  3. Complete your first failure-log entry

Before the Session

  • How a language model produces text — prediction, not lookup (covered in this morning's lecture)
  • Machine learning vs. deep learning — definitions and key differences
  • Gerlinger et al. Intratumour heterogeneity and branched evolution revealed by multiregion sequencing. NEJM 2012. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1113205 — this paper is submitted to QED Science at the start of the session
Before the session: Register for Perplexity and QED Science using your institutional email. At the very start of the session, upload the Gerlinger paper to QED Science and let it run in the background — it takes ~20 minutes.

The Activity

This session is the hands-on half of the simulation day. Students work through three AI tool families back-to-back — an evidence-retrieval tool, a reasoning-critique tool, and a custom assistant they build themselves — and compare what each can and cannot catch. By the end, they have experienced the full "trust-but-verify" loop on real medical questions and recorded their first failure-log entry.
